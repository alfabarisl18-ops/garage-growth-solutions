## Exact narration

### 0:00-0:15 - Introduction

Hi, this is Alpha Barrie from Bay State Auto Growth. This is a fictional before-and-after website audit for Bay & Beacon Auto Care. It demonstrates what I look for during a free shop audit; it is not a real client result.

### 0:15-0:50 - Priority one: generic first impression

Here is the intentionally flawed sample before. The page identifies the business, but the first message is generic: trusted service, quality work, and customer satisfaction. It does not tell a visitor that the shop is in East Boston, explain how the repair experience is different, or give someone a strong reason to choose it. The main button says Learn more when a repair customer is more likely trying to call or request service.

### 0:50-1:22 - Priority two: unclear services and missing proof

The service section creates more work for the customer. Broad labels like Auto Repair, Engine Work, and Other Services do not explain which problems the shop can solve. The design is cluttered, and there is no visible review proof, warranty, certification, or useful explanation of how recommendations and approvals work. The visitor has to accept generic claims without enough evidence.

### 1:22-1:50 - Priority three: buried contact path

The phone number, hours, address, and contact form finally appear near the bottom. That means a customer who is ready to act must scroll through most of the page before finding the basic information needed to call or visit. The form is also generic and does not ask about the vehicle or the type of service needed.

### 1:50-2:20 - Mobile failure

On a phone, the problem becomes more serious. The page stays more than one thousand pixels wide instead of adapting to the three-hundred-seventy-five-pixel screen. The customer has to scroll sideways, the navigation is cut off, and there is no visible mobile call button. A ready-to-book visitor should not have to fight the layout just to contact the shop.

### 2:20-2:50 - Improved first screen

Now compare that with the improved fictional demo. The first screen immediately says independent auto repair in East Boston. The headline communicates a clear experience: clear answers and reliable repairs. The copy explains how recommendations are handled, the hours are visible, and the customer can either request service or call the shop without searching.

### 2:50-3:17 - Clear services and trust signals

The improved service section describes actual customer problems: warning lights, brake concerns, scheduled maintenance, suspension issues, heating and air conditioning, and state inspections. The rating, review count, sample warranty, and certification are clearly labeled as demonstration content, but they show where a real shop's verified trust signals should appear.

### 3:17-3:37 - A visible repair process

The repair process also reduces uncertainty. It tells the customer what happens after the request, when the inspection occurs, when pricing is explained, and that the customer approves the work. The sample testimonials are clearly disclosed and demonstrate where real customer proof would support that message.

### 3:37-3:54 - Improved mobile experience

On the same phone width, the improved page fits without horizontal scrolling. The headline remains readable, Request service is easy to tap, and the Call the shop action stays visible at the bottom of the screen.

### 3:54-4:00 - Invitation

That is the path from weak trust to a clear service request. Visit garage-growth-solutions.pages.dev to request your free audit.

## Recording checklist

- Use a quiet room and headset or microphone.
- Start speaking when the on-screen timer begins.
- Keep the tone conversational; do not rush to fill every pause.
- Re-record a sentence if it runs into the next scene, then edit the voice track rather than changing the visual timing.
- Do not describe the comparison as a real client result.
- Do not show browser bookmarks, personal email, passwords, or customer information.
