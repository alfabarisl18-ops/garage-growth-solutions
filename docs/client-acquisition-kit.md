# Client acquisition kit

## Ideal first client

Target independent general-repair, tire, body, or specialty shops in Greater Boston that have one to three locations and at least three visible opportunities:

- unclear or outdated website;
- weak mobile call or quote path;
- incomplete or inconsistent service information;
- few recent reviews or little owner response activity;
- old or low-quality photos;
- strong real-world reputation that is not visible online;
- an owner or manager who can approve changes.

Avoid franchises, dealerships, obviously closed businesses, shops already using a strong agency system, and businesses where the owner cannot be identified after reasonable research.

## Personalized email

Subject: Quick observation about [Shop Name]

Hi [First Name],

I’m Alpha Barrie, founder of Bay State Auto Growth. I help independent auto shops in Greater Boston improve the online trust signals that influence calls and booked work.

I noticed [one specific, accurate observation] when looking at [Shop Name] online. The shop appears to have [one honest positive], but [customer-facing gap] may make the next step harder for a new customer.

I can send you a free one-page review with three priority findings and a short video. There is no obligation. Would that be useful?

Alpha Barrie
Bay State Auto Growth
[Your physical mailing address]

Reply “no” and I will not follow up.

## First phone call

> Hi, is [owner/manager name] available? My name is Alpha Barrie. I help independent Greater Boston auto shops improve how they look and convert online. I noticed one specific issue on [Shop Name’s website/Google profile]: [brief observation]. I’m not calling to sell ads. I’d like to send a free one-page review with three practical findings. Would you be open to seeing it?

If interested, confirm the best email and ask permission to follow up after delivery. If not interested, thank them and close the record.

## In-person opener

> Hi, I’m Alpha Barrie. I work with independent auto shops on website trust, Google Profile clarity, and review systems. I was looking at how local customers find [Shop Name] and noticed [specific observation]. I made a brief note about it. Who is the best person to send a free three-point review to?

Keep the visit under five minutes unless the owner invites a longer conversation. Never interrupt active service work or approach during an obvious rush.

## Follow-up 1 — three business days later

> Hi [First Name], following up on the free review I offered for [Shop Name]. I would focus on [finding one], [finding two], and the path customers use to [call/request service]. If you want it, I can send the one-page review and short video this week.

## Follow-up 2 — five business days later

> Hi [First Name], I’ll close the loop after this message. If improving [specific issue] becomes a priority, I’m happy to prepare the free review. Either way, no response is needed.

Do not continue automated follow-ups after an opt-out or clear lack of interest.

## Friday review

Ask:

1. Which observation received the most responses?
2. Which channel reached the most owners?
3. Where did prospects stop moving forward?
4. Did audit production stay under 20 minutes?
5. What should be changed once before next week rather than reinvented for every prospect?
